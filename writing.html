Morning Wisdom · August 12, 2026

Peace

In the Christian tradition, peace as a Fruit of the Spirit is something deeper than simply having no trouble around you. The New Testament word often translated as peace, eirēnē, carries ideas of wholeness and well-being. It is possible, then, to be surrounded by unfinished things and still possess peace.

A companion thought from Stoic philosophy

Epictetus repeatedly returned to the distinction between what belongs to our control—our judgments, choices, and responses—and what does not.

Christian peace and Stoic tranquility aren't the same thing. One is ultimately grounded in relationship with God; the other emerges from disciplined judgment about our place in nature. But they can offer remarkably compatible advice on an ordinary Wednesday morning:

You don't have to control everything around you to keep everything around you from controlling you.

There will always be another problem to solve. Another notification. Another thing somebody needs. Another wonderfully ill-advised project that seemed very reasonable when conceived at 11:47 p.m.

Peace doesn't require finishing the world before breakfast.

Sometimes it is simply deciding that, for this moment, what is here is enough.

On this day — August 12

1960 — Humanity bounced a conversation off a giant balloon in space. NASA launched Echo 1, an enormous aluminum-coated balloon that reflected radio waves back toward Earth. The experiment helped demonstrate that long-distance communication through space was possible.

I adore the simplicity of that. Before humanity built sophisticated telecommunications networks in orbit, some engineers essentially said, “What if we put an enormous shiny ball up there and bounced the radio off it?”

1981 — The personal-computer age received one of its defining machines. IBM unveiled the Personal Computer 5150. The base machine had 16 kilobytes of RAM, and its relatively open architecture encouraged other companies to build software and hardware around it.

Behold. The future.

And they would have been right.

Something to carry today

Echo 1 didn't need to create the message.
It only needed to reflect it faithfully.

The first IBM PC didn't contain everything computers would become.
It only needed to make room for other people to build.

Perhaps peace works a little like that.

You don't need to contain the whole future today. Just make enough quiet inside yourself for the next good thing to arrive.

The world can wait until you've had your coffee. ☕🌸

Morning Wisdom · August 11, 2026

Kindness

In the Christian tradition, kindness is counted among the Fruit of the Spirit—not merely politeness, but a disposition toward actively seeking another person's good.

A companion thought from Confucian philosophy

There is an interesting companion to this in ren (仁), often translated as humaneness or benevolence. Confucian thought treats good character as something revealed and cultivated in our relationships with the people around us.

They aren't identical ideas. Christian kindness grows from a particular understanding of God and grace, while ren belongs to a broader Confucian vision of cultivating human character and harmonious relationships. Yet they meet in a lovely place:

The quality of a life is partly revealed by what happens to other people when they encounter it.

Kindness needn't be enormous today. Hold a door. Give someone the benefit of the doubt. Tell somebody they did a good job. Let an irritation pass unanswered.

Small things have surprisingly long shadows.

On this day — August 11

1964 — Beatlemania hit American movie theaters. A Hard Day's Night had its U.S. premiere on August 11, during the extraordinary first wave of Beatlemania in America.

1965 — And exactly one year later, they did it again. The Beatles' second film, Help!, opened widely in the United States on August 11, sending John, Paul, George and Ringo through a wonderfully ridiculous globe-trotting comedy.

Sometimes the thing you create for fun lasts much longer than anyone expects.

Go leave the world a little kinder than you found it today. 🌸
